# AI Agent Ecosystem Site — Content Plan (v2)
*Derived from the taxonomy in `planning/taxonomy/` (one file per section since 2026-09-13; the last single-file version was v2.19). This is a planning document for the site itself — not a mirror of the taxonomy doc. Supersedes v1; see "Changes from v1" at the bottom for what moved and why. Goals, non-goals, success criteria, and MVP prioritization live in `site-prd.md`, not here — this doc owns architecture and content organization only.*

## 1. Purpose & audience

The site is the living evolution of the taxonomy doc, not a copy of it. The taxonomy doc was scaffolding to get here; once published, the site is the source of truth and grows on its own. **Primary audience: reference material for the author and other people who already build agents, or are learning to.** Recruiters, hiring managers, and other AI-forward practitioners may end up viewing it too, but the site isn't designed around them — this corrects an earlier assumption in this doc that treated the cold-reader/recruiter case as primary.

**Consequence of this correction:** the cold-reader narrative entry page (§8 item 3) stays a legitimate nice-to-have for whoever encounters the site incidentally, but it's no longer a design-driving concern. The site can lean fully into reference-lookup patterns — deep permalinks, fast navigation to one specific concept, minimal ceremony before the content — without needing to also work as a stranger's first introduction to the space.

**Scope boundary: AI Transformation content stays a separate future site, not a pillar here.** Considered and decided against folding it in, even though the topics are related and both stem from the same personal learning effort. The deciding factor was voice and format, not topic distance: this site's whole design converges on neutral, reference-style, scannable content (accordions, ordered concept lists, product catalogs), while AI Transformation content is naturally narrative and opinionated — a different genre, not just a different subject wearing the same template. Revisit if AI Transformation content turns out to actually want the same reference-catalog shape (a taxonomy of adoption frameworks or maturity models, say) rather than narrative/strategic writing — that would flip the call. Full non-goals treatment lives in the PRD (queued, not yet drafted); this is the short version so the decision isn't only recorded in conversation.

## 2. Format: hybrid

Every top-level section (Build, Connect, Run) opens with a short framing paragraph — what this category means, why it's shaped this way — then drops into reference-style content: scannable entries, minimal prose per item. Budget: roughly 60–80 words per stage intro, plus a landing page.

**Control is budgeted separately, not folded into the same word count.** Because Control's hub page (see §5) has to justify a non-obvious IA decision — why these concerns are scattered across three stages instead of grouped as a fourth — it needs real explanatory content, likely 200–300 words on its own. This was a genuine budget conflict in v1 and is resolved by giving Control its own line item rather than sharing the ~70-word-per-section allowance.

## 3. Narrative treatment, extended to the whole taxonomy — and the resulting page architecture

The what/why/when narrative treatment developed for the Control policy hub isn't staying scoped to Control. **It applies throughout — to all fifteen top-level sections of the taxonomy** (twelve became fifteen once §2 and §8 were each split into their component parts — see "Changes from v1" and the taxonomy doc's own v2.9 changelog entry), **including a corresponding pass in the taxonomy doc itself where it makes sense**, not just as intro paragraphs on the site. Each section gets real conceptual explanation: what this category of thing is, why it exists, when/how it's applied, where it's heading — a fixed five-part shape (What/Why/When/How/Where), not loosely adapted per section (see the taxonomy doc's own Formatting rules for the locked-in wording).

This is a narrative/reference split, not a narrative-replaces-reference move — it maps onto the Diátaxis documentation model (explanation vs. reference as distinct content types that stay separate but densely cross-linked), which is exactly the pattern that emerged independently through the Policies hub discussion. Two consequences follow from applying it consistently:

**Terminology: "Landscape," not "Tools."** A generic "Tools" heading repeated across many subsections collides on click-target labels and is also inaccurate — §4 is models, §14 has policies and processes, not just installable software. **"Landscape" is the standing term for the attributed list of actual named things** (products, models, companies) in any given subsection, always prefixed with the subsection name to keep it unique and clickable — e.g. "Harnesses Landscape," "Frameworks Landscape," "Models Landscape" — never a bare "Tools" link repeated across pages.

**Page shape, per subsection: three tiers, not two.** This reverses the "Rejected alternative" from the original version of this plan, which argued against one-page-per-concept for a cold-reader audience that needed to read a section start-to-finish. That's no longer the primary audience (§1 above) — practitioners doing reference lookups get more value from deep, bookmarkable permalinks than from a smooth single-page read, and the platform (Quartz) is built for exactly this pattern of many small interlinked notes. The reversal:
- **The narrative page** (e.g. `/build/agent-harnesses`) carries only the What/Why/When/How/Where explanation — no inline concept content anymore. Changes rarely; this is where conceptual understanding of the category as a whole lives.
- **Concept-grouping pages** (e.g. `/build/orchestration-patterns/routing`, `/build/orchestration-patterns/agent-handoffs`, `/build/agent-behavior/policies`) — one page per named item in a section's existing list, at whatever grain the taxonomy doc already gives it: a bundle with children (e.g. "Reasoning strategies," holding CoT/ReAct/Tree-of-Thought/Self-consistency under one page) gets one page for the whole bundle, and a standalone leaf with no children (e.g. "Routing," "Subagents") gets its own page too — the taxonomy doc's own list structure is the page boundary, not a separate design exercise. This is the piece that reverses the old decision: edit isolation (adding, removing, or rewriting one concept never touches anything else) now outweighs the old fragmentation concern, given the audience shift. Pages are created incrementally, as content for each is actually written — this is not a complete set to scaffold upfront, and a section sitting at zero concept-grouping pages for a while is normal, not a gap. Whether a bundle page (e.g. "Reasoning strategies") later fragments into individual per-item pages is an open, low-stakes question left for whenever that content gets written — the hierarchy can always grow one level more granular later by nesting new links inside an existing page, without deciding that now.
- **The landscape page** — the Commercial/Proprietary, Open Source/Provider-agnostic, and Legacy/Decommissioned axis is universal and never itself triggers a page split: every Landscape page (or Landscape sub-page, see below) is structured by that same three-way axis, and if one bucket is empty for a given category, that's stated explicitly ("no commercial tools currently exist in this category") rather than the label being silently dropped. What *does* trigger a page split is a genuine product-category difference already present in the taxonomy doc — e.g. Knowledge and Retrieval's Landscape splits into its own **Vector Databases**, **Knowledge Graphs**, and **Semantic Layer Tools** pages (e.g. `/connect/knowledge-and-retrieval/vector-databases`), each internally structured by Commercial/OSS/Legacy; the same category-first split applies to Model Infrastructure (Inference Providers, Model Serving, Model Routers/Gateways/Proxies, Local Inference) and Tools and Environment Interfaces (MCP Registries, Browser Automation, Voice/Multimodal Tools, Generative UI Tools), and to Agent Frameworks (Code Frameworks, No-code / Low-code Builders — taxonomy v2.19). A section with only one product category (Harnesses, Behavior and Configuration) has nothing to split on and stays one Landscape page, itself structured by the Commercial/OSS/Legacy axis. Order of operations: category split first (if the taxonomy doc's Landscape already names more than one), then Commercial/OSS/Legacy within whatever page results — never the other way around, and the axis itself is never the split. Whether a whole section ever needs to split into multiple top-level *sections* over this same category question is a separate, bigger call, deliberately not addressed here.
- **Landscape pages can be scaffolded ahead of their content, unlike concept pages** — a real, deliberate divergence, not something to normalize away. It's fine for a Landscape or Landscape sub-page to exist as a stub to be filled in later; this holds even where the resulting page is thin (e.g. Knowledge Graphs, with only two entries) — a thin catalog is still a useful signal about the state of that market. The one exception: a section or category with genuinely no tooling at all — Orchestration Patterns, Context — gets no Landscape page or tier at all, not even an empty stub. Scaffolding-ahead is for content that will eventually exist; it isn't a reason to create a page for something that structurally doesn't need one. Landscape pages stay separate, linked-out pages holding only the attributed product/tool list — reference-style, scannable, no prose — unchanged from the original reasoning: they change on a different cadence than everything else and stay isolated so market updates never risk breaking conceptual content.
- **Visual treatment:** the What/Why/When/How/Where beats stay as in-place expandable accordions (a reader stays on the page). Landscape links are navigation, not expansion, and render as prominent cards below the accordions — one card per Landscape page for that section, so a section with a single combined Landscape shows one card and a section whose Landscape splits by sub-category (per the granularity rule above) shows one card per sub-category — it's the comprehensive catalog either way, so these stay the most visually weighted elements on the page. Concept-grouping links are also navigation, but render as a plain ordered list rather than a card grid: a wrapping grid has no legible reading order, while a single-column list makes top-to-bottom order unambiguous without needing to label it — no rank numbers shown, position alone conveys relative order. This holds even when the list is incomplete, which per the incremental-accumulation bullet above is the normal case, not an edge case: the visible items still appear in their correct order relative to each other, even though their position on the page (1st, 2nd, 3rd shown) isn't their absolute rank in the section's full priority sequence — a reader sees "these come before those," not "this is priority #4 of 15." Each row is quieter than the Landscape card: a title plus a short one-line description of its children, or "single concept" for a leaf with none — no border, no box. Either tier is silently omitted when it doesn't apply to a section — no placeholder, no visible empty slot, no explanatory note — and the page goes straight from the accordions to whichever of the two tiers does apply, or ends at the accordions if neither does. This is a single uniform rule for both tiers, not a concept-specific one: a section can lack concept-grouping pages (not yet written), lack a Landscape entirely (Orchestration Patterns, Context — no tooling exists), or lack both.
- **Concept-grouping pages accumulate over time, not upfront.** At any given point a section may have zero, some, or eventually one page per item in its list — that's normal ongoing authoring, not a design gap to close before launch. The one genuine, permanent exception: sections with no separate concept list at all — all their content lives in the five narrative beats and the Landscape, nothing in between — never get this tier. That's Models and Model Infrastructure (content is just the Landscape itself, nothing else), and also Agent Harnesses and Agent Frameworks (their content lives entirely in the narrative beats — for Harnesses, ADE was deliberately folded into How/Where rather than left as a list item — with nothing left over to make a concept list from). "Pure landscape" was the wrong description for this exception: Harnesses in particular has the most fully-drafted narrative in the whole doc, it just has no separate concept list alongside it.
- **Concept-grouping pages are ordered, not alphabetical or arbitrary.** Display order follows the taxonomy doc's own bullet order for that section, top to bottom — and the taxonomy doc's bullet order is itself now meaningful: high-level, high-leverage concepts first, narrower or more niche ones later, the order a newcomer would actually want to read in. This makes the taxonomy doc the single source of truth for both content and reading order, rather than maintaining a separate curated display order that could drift out of sync. Reordering happens per-section as time allows, not all at once — §5 Agent Behavior and Configuration is the first section to get this treatment (taxonomy doc v2.10); sections not yet reordered still just reflect drafting order until they are. Sub-items within one grouping (e.g. Prompts vs. Skills within Instructions) aren't reordered yet either — an open, deferred decision, not one made against it.
- **Every page carries a last-modified date and read time under its title**, in the same format sitewide — e.g. `Sep 07, 2026, 2 min read`. This applies uniformly across all three tiers (narrative, concept-grouping, Landscape), and reflects that specific page's own last edit, not the taxonomy doc as a whole — a section's narrative page, one of its concept-grouping pages, and its Landscape page can each show a different date, since each is its own file.

This changes the site's page count and nav depth beyond what §4 (Navigation structure) originally assumed — a section can now grow to as many pages as it has list items (the narrative page, plus one page per concept-grouping — however many that turns out to be, built up incrementally), plus one Landscape page per genuine product category the taxonomy doc's Landscape already names (not always a single page, and these can be scaffolded ahead of their content rather than only incrementally) — and needs to be factored into the Quartz file rework alongside the slug renaming and Control migration, none of which has been physically done yet.

**Standard narrative template: What / Why / When / How / Where, plus a linked Landscape.** Five `###` sub-headers, in that order, roughly repeated across every section rather than freely adapted each time: **What** (definition, including cross-references to adjacent concepts a reader might confuse it with), **Why** (the problem this category solves), **When** (decision criteria for reaching for it, including risk factors like vendor lock-in), **How** (how it's actually used in practice), **Where** (which direction the category is heading). Then a `### <Section> Landscape` sub-header holding the Commercial/Open Source/Legacy lists, as its own linked page. This revises the earlier framing ("adapted per section rather than forced into an identical five-beat template everywhere") — the five-part shape is now the default, not just a loose analogy to the Policies hub arc.

**Worked example: §1 Agent Harnesses** (renamed from "Agent Products and Harnesses" — "Harnesses" alone matches the Landscape link name and the narrative content, which is entirely about harnesses, not a broader "products" concept). This is the first section actually rebuilt to the five-part shape in the taxonomy doc itself. Use it as the template for the other fourteen sections rather than re-deriving the shape each time.

**Cross-cutting concepts that aren't their own product category.** Building §1 out fully surfaced three reusable principles: (1) a mode several existing products share rather than a separate product line (e.g. ADE, which Cursor/Devin Desktop/Zencoder all have) gets folded into whichever existing narrative beat it belongs to — ADE landed under **How**, with its origin story under **Where** — plus the existing `ADE` tag for cross-cutting filtering and light cross-reference notes on the Landscape entries that have it, never a duplicate listing. The reverse case follows from the same test: once a mode spins out into a genuinely separate product, it gets its own Landscape entry in whichever section it now fits, with reciprocal cross-references — Warp's ADE capability became Warp Factories, listed in §13 Agent Operations and Deployment (taxonomy v2.14); (2) a concept genuinely distinct from things already covered elsewhere (e.g. "agent framework" vs. "agent harness") earns a one-line cross-reference under **What**, not a full explanation duplicated in both places; (3) a real risk factor relevant to the category (e.g. vendor lock-in) belongs under **When**, grounded in concrete evidence already in the doc where it exists, rather than left implicit. Apply the test in (1) to any future cross-cutting concept before giving it its own section: does it name a distinct thing, or a mode/feature of things already listed elsewhere?

## 4. Navigation structure

- **Top-level nav:** Build → Connect → Run (shown as a pipeline/flow), plus **Control** as a separate tab explicitly marked as cross-cutting — not a fourth sequential stage.
- **Sub-nav:** section pages under Build/Connect/Run. **Numbering is dropped from site navigation and URLs; use descriptive slugs instead** (e.g. `/build/harnesses`, `/connect/model-infrastructure`), even though the taxonomy doc keeps its internal §1–15 numbering as a reference-document convention. Numbers are a print affordance — on a site where content moves between pages as the taxonomy evolves, continuous numbering guarantees renumbering churn every time something is reorganized, which is exactly what the Control migration below does. This is a bigger implementation lift than it sounds: no Quartz install exists yet at all, so this means scaffolding the site fresh with slug-based paths from the start, rather than the simpler "rename existing files" framing this line used to have — see "Next step" at the bottom.
- **Connect's sub-nav order diverges from the taxonomy doc's own §6–11 sequence, on purpose.** Site nav order: Model Infrastructure → Tools and Environment Interfaces → Context → Memory → Knowledge and Retrieval → Agent Communication and Interoperability — prioritizing what's foundational (model access, then tool-calling, on par with each other) before the cognitive-augmentation progression (Context → Memory → Knowledge and Retrieval builds on itself) and leaving Agent Communication, the most specialized/multi-agent-specific topic, last. This is exactly the case the numbering-vs-slugs decision above was made for: because nav uses slugs, not §-numbers, the site can reorder how sections are presented without renumbering the taxonomy doc at all — the taxonomy doc's internal §6–11 sequence stays untouched as its own reference-document convention, and only the site's display order differs.
- **Tags:** cross-cutting filters — already auto-generated by Quartz. Adding Security, Governance, and Eval tags (see `planning/taxonomy/tags.md`) to reflect content that's about to scatter across three stage pages; without them, there's no way to see "everything security-related" as a set once it's no longer grouped under one section.
- **Home page and how-to-use page:** the home page is the site root (§9), linked implicitly as the logo/title rather than needing its own nav tab. The how-to-use-this-site page (§9) gets a small, low-emphasis link — not a top-level tab alongside Build/Connect/Run/Control, since it's orientation for newcomers rather than reference content itself; likely footer or a corner link near the nav, exact placement TBD when the nav is actually built.
- **Cloud AI Platforms hub:** nested in Connect's sub-nav, near Model Infrastructure — not a top-level tab (§8 item 1 has the full reasoning).

## 5. The Control redesign

Control is genuinely cross-cutting — a security policy applies at build time, an eval harness watches at runtime, governance touches deployment. Treating it as "step 4" misrepresented that. Resolution: **split placement, keep browsability.**

- **Canonical content lives where it's governed.** Each tool/concept gets one home page under whichever stage it actually operates in. This includes Landscape sub-category pages created by the Landscape-splitting rule (§3) — e.g. Prompt-Injection Defense Tools becomes a page under Connect, not a page on the Control hub, once its parent concept moves there.
- **Control becomes a hub page**, not a stage. It carries real explanatory content — why these concerns are cross-cutting, how to read the hub — not just a bare index, plus links out to where each governed item actually lives, grouped by which stage they govern. This applies to scattered Landscape pages exactly as it does to concepts: the hub surfaces a rolled-up "at a glance" index of every governance-relevant Landscape tool, organized by which stage they now live under, with each entry wikilinking back to its one canonical page — so a visitor can see everything security/governance/eval-relevant from the hub without any tool list being duplicated or copied.
- **Genuinely global items** — the ones that don't cleanly attach to one stage even after this pass — get their own content directly on the Control hub.

### Migration mapping (revised)

**Moves into Build:**
- Agent and Prompt Governance — specifically Prompt and instruction governance (system instructions, agent instructions, prompt versioning, approval/review, prompt testing) — pairs with existing Agent Behavior and Configuration content. **Not the same taxonomy bullet as Policy instructions below**, despite the similar name: Instruction hierarchy and Provenance/change tracking are Policy instructions' own children, not this bullet's, and stay on the Control hub per the Policies hub split — they don't move to Build.
- Model/tool policies — moves out of Control to sit as a sibling alongside Policies in Build; see "Policies hub" decision below for why the content/lifecycle split puts both policy types here
- The Guardrails tool list itself is already canonically in Build; Control's Policy enforcement entry becomes a pure cross-reference, not a second copy of the tool list

**Moves into Connect:**
- Access and Authorization — permissions, agent identity mechanism (WorkOS, Auth0), authentication/authorization, capability-based access
  - **Exception: Agent identity specifically stays global on the hub, not Connect.** Identity is issued at deploy time, presented at connect time, and attributed at audit time — it doesn't have one home the way the authorization mechanism does.
- Secrets management (Doppler, Vault, Infisical) and credential delegation
- Prompt-injection defenses (Lakera, CalypsoAI) — the dominant injection vector is untrusted content arriving through tools, web fetches, email, and MCP servers; that's the Connect boundary, not the execution edge
  - MCP-specific attack surface (tool poisoning, tool-description injection, confused-deputy, rug-pull updates) moves here as a sibling entry, not a separate governance block — same Connect boundary, but a distinct threat model (protocol/supply-chain trust vs. content-level injection), so the two stay adjacent but not merged

**Moves into Run:**
- Isolation and Infrastructure Security — dissolved at the taxonomy-doc level (v2.11), not migrated as a block: its three items were individually redistributed into §12's own General Concepts rather than merged wholesale into the Sandboxes page. Sandboxing had no content beyond a pointer to §12 and needed nothing new; Isolation became a new §12 concept item with no prior counterpart; Network restrictions became its own new §12 item too, cross-referenced to the existing Network access rather than merged into it, since the two are related but not identical (runtime capability vs. the security policy constraining it)
- Rate limiting and Resource/budget limits (split out of "Audit and Resource Controls" — see below)

**Stays global on the Control hub:**
- Data Governance (classification, PII protection, DLP, access controls, retention) — this is the one block in §14 with no tools at all, and it fails the single-stage test more clearly than anything else: data is governed at connectors, at context assembly (Build), and in traces/logs (Run) — observability data is one of the largest PII surfaces in an agent system and one of the most commonly missed if this gets buried under Connect
- Agent identity (see exception above)
- Audit logs (split out of "Audit and Resource Controls") — the compliance record's reader is a governance reader, not an ops reader, unlike rate limiting/budget caps which are genuinely operational
- Human Oversight — approval policies, human-in-the-loop, escalation (spans design-time workflow design and live escalation)
- **Policies hub.** Titled and slugged as "Policies hub" specifically — not bare "Policies" — because Build already has its own concept page titled exactly "Policies" (the taxonomy bullet holding Behavioral and Model/tool scope), and Quartz's flat wikilink namespace can't resolve two pages with the same name. Resolves the four overlapping "policy" terms as two different kinds of thing, not four types: policy *content* (Behavioral and Model/tool scope — both live in Build as siblings under Policies, since they're both rules defined at configuration time, just different subjects — conduct vs. capability) versus policy *lifecycle* (Policy instructions and Policy enforcement — generic process stages that apply to any policy regardless of subject, and stay here on the Control hub). The hub page itself carries the conceptual "what are policies and why" framing plus a description of the two content types at a high level, then links down to Build's "Policies" page for the actual rule content; Build's Policies page links back up here for the "why does this exist" framing. Narrative arc for this hub (dense version, confirmed): ground the need for policies in agents taking real autonomous action → introduce the two types at a conceptual level with links out to Build → cover Policy instructions (authoring/review/approval/versioning) → cover Policy enforcement (runtime application, ties to Guardrails tooling in Build) → close on the feedback loop, incidents/new requirements re-entering the same instructions-then-enforcement pipeline rather than requiring new infrastructure.
- **All of §15 — Observability, Evaluation, and Debugging/Optimization, together.** These three share the same tools (Langfuse, Braintrust, Arize, W&B Weave do observability *and* evals), so splitting them across a stage page and the hub would force those tool entries to be either duplicated or orphaned — breaking the "one canonical home per tool" principle on the very first section it's applied to. Keeping all of §15 together also gives the hub enough substantive content to function as a real destination, not just a router page.
